How to Build Your First Master Schedule

This is a live learning guide. Instead of just reading, you will be building. Open this workspace page in one window on your monitor, and open your active Aeries database in another window alongside it. We will proceed chronologically to configure and audit your database structures from scratch before scheduling a single student.

Your Active Build Strategy

Building a schedule requires absolute precision. We are going to treat every step as a live design session. By entering sections and constraints sequentially, you prevent system logic errors during the student loading phase. Let's align your database before making changes.

Our Goal:

By the end of this guide, you will have configured terms, balanced school sizing counts, mapped out teacher FTE constraints, and organized clean student requests in your real database window.

The Live Checklist We Are Creating

  • Phase 1: Align calendar deadlines and school day hours.
  • Phase 2: Match class sizing counts with our staffing.
  • Phase 3: Build structural sections (singletons first!).
  • Phase 4: Resolve duplicate or broken requests.

Understanding the Aeries Tables We are Modifying

As you work inside Aeries, you are directly interacting with specific database tables. Let's look at the three primary tables we will be configuring and auditing together:

STU (Student Data Table)

Stores who the students are. This includes their base demographic information, grade level, and enrollment status.

SSS (Student Requests Table)

Stores what classes they want. This lists the specific course codes requested by families or counselors.

MST / SMS (Master Schedule Table)

Stores where/when classes occur. This houses the physical structures: teachers, periods, rooms, and seat caps.

The 8 Beginner Training Milestones

Work through these eight steps systematically. Do not skip steps. Let's begin.

Step 1 — Map Out Your Timeline Off-Screen

Beginner Milestone 1 • Preparation Strategy

Goal: Before touching your live database, define who has the final authority to make curriculum decisions and know when your drafts are due.

Where to go in your workspace:

Check your email inbox or local school site documentation folders for the district's Educational Services calendar.

What we are doing together:

  1. Identify the target launch date when families should see schedules.
  2. Mark an "internal review" checkpoint on your calendar one week prior to release.
  3. Determine who approves exceptions (e.g., if a student requests two overlapping singletons, who resolves it?).
Teacher Tip: Scheduling issues usually occur because administrators make changes during busy enrollment days without documenting them. Establish a "Change Request Form" early.

Step 2 — Configure the School Bell Structure

Beginner Milestone 2 • Navigating Time Settings

Goal: Set up and verify how Aeries tracks instructional time blocks. If these period hours are mapped wrong, student attendance will break.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

School Info / Scheduling → Bell Schedule / Calendar / Terms

What we are doing together:

  1. Open the **Bell Schedule** module. Compare it to your paper bell schedule sheet.
  2. Verify that each period number (including Period 0 or Advisory) matches.
  3. Ensure the AM/PM flags are set correctly for each block.
  4. Check the calendar terms: Standard Semesters (S1/S2) or Quarters (Q1-Q4).
Beginner Mistake: Entering an advisory or supervision block as a regular class period. If you do this, Aeries will attempt to generate grades for it. Keep non-instructional blocks clearly flagged.

Step 3 — Project Your Grade-Level Sizing

Beginner Milestone 3 • Section Calculation

Goal: Calculate exactly how many sections (classes) we need to open for each grade. This prevents over-hiring or having overcrowded classes.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Reports → Enrollment Reports / Student Lists / Grade-Level Counts

Try out this section calculator:

Calculated Minimum Required Sections: 5.0 Sections
Important Rule of Sizing: If your equation yields 5.2, you *must* open 6 sections, or coordinate with district personnel to adjust class cap exceptions.

Step 4 — Verify Staffing Allowances (FTE)

Beginner Milestone 4 • Auditing Teacher Availability

Goal: Make sure teachers aren't assigned to teach more periods than their contracts allow, and that split-site (itinerant) teachers are available during scheduled times.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling → Teachers / Staff Assignments

Beginner Verification Checks:

  • Open each teacher's record. Check their assigned FTE limit (e.g., 1.0 FTE is standard, 0.5 FTE is half-time).
  • Confirm each teacher's designated prep period. Mark it on a spreadsheet as "unavailable".
  • Verify that itinerant music or PE staff are not scheduled at your school when they are assigned to another site.

Step 5 — Identify Protected Singletons First

Beginner Milestone 5 • Immovable Blocks

Goal: Locate classes that only meet once per day. If singletons are built last, they will clash with other classes, creating a scheduling bottleneck.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling → Courses / Sections
What is a Singleton? Common examples include AP Chemistry, Leadership, Advanced Band, or Specialized SPED push-ins. Because these only meet at one specific hour, students have zero flexibility to choose alternative sections.
Pro-Tip: Write down all your school's singletons on a sticky note. Place these classes on your master scheduling board *first* before assigning general English, Math, or History sections.

Step 6 — Clean Up Bad Student Requests

Beginner Milestone 6 • Pre-Scheduling Database Audit

Goal: Find and repair incomplete, duplicate, or missing student requests inside the SSS database table before initiating scheduling runs.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling → Course Requests / Student Requests

Verify these steps:

  1. Run a report of "Students Missing Course Requests" to catch students who haven't selected electives.
  2. Check for duplicates (e.g., a student requested English 9 twice).
  3. Verify that incoming students from feeder elementary or middle schools have clean records in the system.

Step 7 — Build Your First Schedule Draft

Beginner Milestone 7 • Constructing Sections

Goal: Manually enter your draft sections into the SMS (Scheduling Master Schedule) table in the correct structural order.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling → Master Schedule / Scheduling Master Schedule

Step-by-Step Entry Protocol:

  1. Begin with your singletons and doubletons identified in Step 5.
  2. Next, place special education and English learner support courses.
  3. Follow up with core classes (e.g., English, Math, Science).
  4. Conclude by placing general elective sections that have multiple alternate open hours.

Step 8 — Run Key Audits and Validation Checks

Beginner Milestone 8 • Error Assessment

Goal: Let Aeries scan your draft to check for double-booked classrooms, overloaded teachers, or unresolved student conflicts.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling → Reports / Conflict Reports / Teacher Load

The Top Three Reports to Run:

  • Teacher Load Report: Confirms no teacher is assigned to teach two classes during the same period.
  • Room Overlap Report: Confirms no physical room has two different classes assigned to it at the same hour.
  • Conflict Matrix: Shows the percentage of student schedules that successfully resolved without errors. Aim for above 85% success on your first validation run!
