How to Build Your Middle School Master Schedule

Middle school scheduling is a puzzle of intersecting demands. Unlike self-contained classrooms, students here rotate between subject-matter experts, work within balanced academic teams, and flow through elective block rotations. Open this training window side-by-side with your active Aeries database to audit and configure your middle school schedule cleanly.

Middle School Core Strategy

The secret to middle school scheduling lies in controlling the flow of student cohorts. When you organize academic teams, establish elective wheels, and configure flex intervention periods in the correct database sequence, you prevent bottleneck conflicts before the computer schedules a single student.

Our Build Goal:

Together, we are going to define Scheduling Groups to link core academic cohorts, build trimester or quarterly rotating elective sections, and setup dynamic WIN support blocks that track clean daily attendance.

The Live Checklist We Are Building

  • Phase 1: Interlock cohorts using Aeries Scheduling Groups.
  • Phase 2: Structure split-term elective wheel blocks.
  • Phase 3: Configure non-GPA intervention/WIN periods.
  • Phase 4: Balance teacher loads and handle shared staff limits.

Understanding the Aeries Tables We are Modifying

Middle school scheduling relies on specific database fields to cluster students. Let's look at the Aeries tables we are modifying together:

STU.SG (Scheduling Group Field)

Stores the cohort tag. By tagging a student cohort and matching sections with this code, Aeries forces those students to stay together in core classes.

SEC.TM (Section Term Code)

Determines when a class is active. For elective wheels, we configure these as Trimesters (T1, T2, T3) or Quarters (Q1-Q4) instead of full Years.

MST.MX / MST.ACT (Limits & Counts)

Houses room seat caps (MX) and current student loads (ACT). We audit these to keep core classes and elective rotations completely balanced.

The 5 Middle School Training Milestones

Perform these configurations systematically in your active database window. Do not skip steps.

Step 1 — Establish Academic Teams (Scheduling Groups)

Middle School Milestone 1 • Cohort Control

Goal: Link specific student cohorts with a designated team of core teachers (e.g., Team 7A ELA/Social Studies and Team 7A Math/Science) so they transition as a unified group.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Student Data (STU) → Scheduling Groups

What we are doing together:

  1. Identify your grade-level cohort blocks (e.g., Team 7A, Team 7B).
  2. Assign a unique numeric Scheduling Group code to the students in Aeries (located in the STU.SG field).
  3. Create core academic sections in the Scheduling Master Schedule (SMS) and assign the matching Scheduling Group code to those sections (SEC.SG).
  4. When you run the scheduler, Aeries will only schedule students from Group 1 into core sections tagged with Group 1.
Teaming Tip: Organizing students into Scheduling Groups reduces schedule conflict checks by over 90% because the algorithm doesn't have to search your entire master list of core classes to place a student.

Step 2 — Configure Split-Term Elective Wheels

Middle School Milestone 2 • Trimester & Quarter Rotations

Goal: Build elective wheels (e.g., Art, Computer Literacy, and Music) where the same student group rotates through a new class each trimester or quarter without manual scheduling changes.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Master Schedule (SMS) → Term Codes

What we are doing together:

  1. Create distinct, unique course codes for each elective option (do not use the same course code for different topics).
  2. Create sections in the SMS with specific term boundaries: use T1, T2, and T3 for trimesters, or Q1 through Q4 for quarters.
  3. Align the period, teacher, and room. For example, if Room 102 holds Art in T1, it can hold Computers in T2, and Music in T3—all scheduled during Period 5.
  4. Keep the seat capacity (MST.MX) identical across all rotating sections to ensure even student loads during trimester changes.
Beginner Mistake: Setting up an elective wheel using standard "Yearlong" course terms and trying to drop/add students manually mid-year. This breaks state reporting and grading records! Always configure real Term split sections (T1/T2/T3) from the start.

Step 3 — Audit Team Enrollment & Section Allocation

Middle School Milestone 3 • Sizing & Staffing Balance

Goal: Ensure you have allocated the correct number of sections per academic team so that class sizes do not exceed district limits.

Try out this Middle School Team Sizer:

Students Per Team: 120 Students
Minimum Sections Required per Core Subject per Team: 4 Sections

What we are doing together:

  1. Input your total grade-level enrollment and divide it by your target team count to verify balanced student blocks.
  2. Ensure that the number of core sections you create in the SMS matches or exceeds the minimum calculated sections.
  3. Check that the teacher’s daily assignment load does not exceed contract agreements (e.g., standard five instructional periods max).

Step 4 — Build Flex Intervention / WIN Blocks

Middle School Milestone 4 • advisory and WIN Setup

Goal: Set up a daily WIN ("What I Need") or Advisory period to handle flexible intervention, tutoring, and social-emotional learning with daily attendance tracking.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

School Info / Scheduling → Courses → Course Table (CRS)

What we are doing together:

  1. Create a distinct course code for Advisory/WIN (e.g., Course Code `9005 - WIN Block`).
  2. In the course properties, configure this course as **Non-Credit / No Grade** so it does not calculate into student GPAs or generate transcript history.
  3. Build sections in the SMS during your designated period (e.g., Period 4).
  4. Enable the attendance flag on these sections so teachers can take roll during this period. This meets state requirements while maintaining a highly flexible daily support block.
Intervention Strategy: If students rotate between intervention groups on different days of the week, assign them to their permanent advisory homeroom teacher in Aeries for daily roll-call. Use local spreadsheets or supplemental groups to track daily roster rotations.

Step 5 — Run Room & Teacher Overlap Audits

Middle School Milestone 5 • Pre-Scheduling Audit

Goal: Catch scheduling conflicts, teacher double-bookings, and room overlaps before running the student scheduling engine.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Reports → Room Overlap Report / Teacher Load

What we are doing together:

  1. Run the **Teacher Load Report** to confirm no core or elective teacher is scheduled to teach two different classes during the same period.
  2. Run the **Room Overlap Report** to ensure specialized rooms (such as science labs, computer rooms, or gyms) are not double-booked.
  3. Verify shared or itinerant teachers (such as band directors who split sites with the elementary school) have empty periods in your SMS that align with their travel times.
Auditing Success: Resolving room and teacher overlaps at this stage prevents scheduling reject errors from failing your student loads.
