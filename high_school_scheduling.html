How to Build Your High School Master Schedule

High school scheduling represents the pinnacle of database building. Unlike lower grade levels where students are grouped into standardized cohorts, high schoolers move along specialized individual paths—navigating graduation targets, AP constraints, CTE sequences, and college criteria. Open this active workspace on one side of your monitor and your live Aeries staging database on the other to audit, build, and test your master schedule systematically.

High School Core Strategy

The secret to high school scheduling lies in data preparation and strict scheduling sequencing. If student request data is unchecked or sections are scheduled in a random sequence, the math behind student assignment will break. Placing singletons first and continuously auditing class section allocations is your key to achieving a scheduling success rate of 85% or higher on your initial scheduling pass.

Our Build Goal:

Together, we are going to clean student request counts, isolate immovable AP/CTE singletons, mathematically calculate required class sections, run the scheduling pass, and systematically resolve post-scheduling rejects.

The Live Checklist We Are Building

  • Phase 1: Run graduation and request sanity audits.
  • Phase 2: Map and protect AP/CTE singleton blocks.
  • Phase 3: Match sectional count capacity with student loads.
  • Phase 4: Resolve student rejects and balance sections.

Understanding the Aeries Tables We are Modifying

As you build a high school master schedule, you are actively manipulating fields across these primary Aeries tables. Let's make sure you know exactly where your data resides:

SSS (Student Course Requests)

Houses students' selected course requests. Cleaning SSS duplicate files or unmatched codes before running the scheduler is essential for success.

REQ (Graduation Requirements status)

Tracks a student's progress toward district graduation credits. We cross-examine this table to protect senior cohort paths.

HIS (Academic History / Transcripts)

Stores past course credits and grades. High school schedulers check HIS data to confirm prerequisite fulfillment for honors or AP levels.

SMS / MST (Scheduling Master Schedule)

The skeleton board holding teacher codes, period blocks, room locations, term boundaries, and seat limit properties (MX).

The 7 High School Training Milestones

Perform these database configurations systematically in your active Aeries staging environment. Do not skip steps.

Step 1 — Run Request Validation and Demographic Sanity Audits

High School Milestone 1 • Data Cleaning

Goal: Locate and repair broken request entries inside the Student Course Requests (SSS) database table before initiating scheduling runs. This prevents the scheduling algorithm from attempting to match non-existent sections.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Course Requests → Request Reports / Student Request Audit

What we are doing together:

  1. Open your Aeries window and run the **Students Missing Course Requests** report. This isolates students with completely blank scheduling files.
  2. Scan student requests for duplicates (e.g., a student mistakenly signed up for English 10 twice). Remove duplicate SSS files immediately.
  3. Run an audit for course codes linked to retired or discontinued curriculum subjects. Switch these records to active courses.
  4. Check that incoming 9th graders from your feeder middle schools have their demographic records fully transferred and updated in the database.
Beginner Mistake: Leaving bad request records in the SSS table with the assumption that the scheduler will ignore them. The computer will actively attempt to schedule bad requests, which artificially locks available seats and triggers widespread scheduling failures.

Step 2 — Identify and Document Your Immovable Singletons

High School Milestone 2 • Constraint Mapping

Goal: Find and isolate "singletons" (classes that meet only once a day, such as AP Physics, Concert Band, or Advanced Welding) and plot them onto your scheduling board first to prevent scheduling conflicts.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Master Schedule Board (or Course Request Tallies)

What we are doing together:

  1. Open your Course Request Tally report. Highlight every course that has a low student request count, requiring only a single class section.
  2. Look for overlaps among student subgroups. For example, check if students requesting AP Calculus BC are also requesting AP Literature.
  3. Construct a physical or digital grid mapping out the periods where singletons will live. Spread your singletons across your school day (e.g., place AP Physics in Period 2, AP Lit in Period 5) to maximize student access.
The Singleton Rule: Always build from least flexible to most flexible. Place your singletons first, doubletons second, and general core courses (like English 9 or Biology) last. If you build general core classes first, your singletons will eventually conflict, forcing you to completely rebuild your schedule.

Step 3 — Audit Course Requests & singleton Conflict Risk

High School Milestone 3 • Section Allocation & Risk Check

Goal: Calculate exactly how many sections you need to open in the master schedule based on student request metrics and verify singleton scheduling risks.

Try out this High School Section & Conflict Auditor:

Calculated Sections Required: 6 Sections
Core Master Board Scheduling Priority: Standard Placement

What we are doing together:

  1. Input course requests from the Aeries Course Tally into the calculator tool above.
  2. Analyze whether the calculated section count fits within your teachers' daily assignment allocations (e.g., 1.0 FTE allows 5 instructional sections).
  3. Verify that classroom seat constraints (MST.MX) in your master schedule sections match your calculated class caps exactly.

Step 4 — Build Career Pathways and AP Sequences

High School Milestone 4 • Pathway Alignment

Goal: Align Career Technical Education (CTE) pathways and advanced academic sequences so students can transition smoothly through multi-tier programs without schedule conflicts.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Master Schedule → Sections / Scheduling Groups

What we are doing together:

  1. Identify pathway cohorts (e.g., Sports Medicine 1, 2, and 3) that are taught by the same teacher.
  2. Ensure that introductory courses do not block advanced capstone courses. For example, if Sports Medicine 3 is a singleton in Period 4, avoid scheduling Sports Medicine 1 sections exclusively during Period 4.
  3. For locked pathways, use Aeries **Scheduling Groups** or **Section Tags** to reserve seats in specific sections for pathway students.
Path Conflict: Scheduling a pathway capstone course in the exact same period as an AP course that pathway students commonly take. Always check student cross-requests before finalizing course placement.

Step 5 — Run the Aeries Student Scheduling Engine

High School Milestone 5 • Automated Simulation Pass

Goal: Initiate your first scheduling pass in your Aeries staging database to run the matching algorithm and generate draft student schedules.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Student Scheduling → Schedule Students

What we are doing together:

  1. Open the **Schedule Students** utility in your database.
  2. Set your execution parameters: select standard parameters to process your main student body, keeping alternate requests enabled.
  3. Run the automated simulation. Aeries will run thousand of iterations, matching student requests from SSS to sections in SMS.
  4. Do not make live schedule modifications or manual class switches until the full simulation pass completes.
The Scheduling Metric Goal: On your first run, aim to resolve at least **80% to 85%** of student schedules without errors. If your percentage is lower, it indicates underlying singleton placement issues or sectional capacity shortages.

Step 6 — Decode and Resolve Reject List Reports

High School Milestone 6 • Post-Scheduler Troubleshooting

Goal: Analyze conflict reject list reports (commonly known as Reject Code D7 or Unscheduled Student lists) and systematically adjust sections to clear scheduling failures.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Reports → Student Reject Analysis (D7)

What we are doing together:

  1. Run the **Student Reject Analysis Listing** report. This details exactly why students failed to receive schedules.
  2. Group your rejects by error types. If you see many students failing to schedule due to a single AP class being full, increase the sectional limit or open another section.
  3. If you see a recurring period conflict (e.g., AP Lit and Concert Band in Period 1), move one section to a different period on your scheduling board and run the scheduler again.
Auditing Success: Focus on resolving pattern conflicts rather than correcting individual student schedules. Shifting a single section on your master scheduling board can instantly resolve conflicts for dozens of students.

Step 7 — Balance Sectional Loads & Lock the Master Schedule

High School Milestone 7 • Balancing & Hand-off

Goal: Rebalance overfilled and underfilled sections across matching periods and lock the final schedule configuration before publishing details to parents and students.

Aeries Path (Find this in your Aeries Window):

Scheduling Process → Master Schedule → Interactive Scheduling Board

What we are doing together:

  1. Open the **Interactive Scheduling Board** to view section enrollment levels side-by-side across periods.
  2. If you have three sections of English 10 in Period 2 with enrollment counts of 35, 34, and 12, work with your counseling team to move students from the overloaded sections into the smaller section.
  3. Verify that teacher daily assignment loads do not exceed contractual limits before publishing finalized schedules.
  4. Lock the master schedule sections. This protects your hard work, preventing automated systems from overwriting your finalized schedule during subsequent runs.
