How to Start Master Scheduling

If you are building a master schedule for the first time — or it has been a year and you need a refresher — start here. This page walks you through what to do first, what to click, what to look for, and what to avoid before moving into the elementary, middle, or high school scheduling pages.

What this page helps you do

This page helps you prepare before you start building sections or scheduling students. Most scheduling problems happen because someone begins entering sections before the bell schedule, staffing, enrollment, rooms, or program needs are clear.

Goal: By the end of this page, you should know what information you need, where to check it, and whether you are ready to build your first draft.

Before you open Aeries

  • Know your scheduling deadline and internal review dates.
  • Have the current or proposed bell schedule available.
  • Have enrollment projections by grade level or course.
  • Have staffing/FTE information and teacher assignments.
  • Know which programs need protected time or special rooms.
  • Know who will review the schedule before it is submitted.

Important note about Aeries paths

Aeries navigation can vary by district setup, user permissions, school type, and whether your site is working in the scheduling area or the live master schedule. The paths below are written as common Aeries workflow language. If a menu name looks slightly different in your Aeries, use the closest matching Scheduling, Reports, Master Schedule, Course Requests, or Student Scheduling area.

Step-by-step: Start in this order

Step 1 — Confirm the scheduling timeline

Purpose: Before you build anything, know when drafts, review meetings, corrections, and final submission are due.

Where to go

Start outside of Aeries first. Check your district calendar, principal communication, Educational Services timeline, or site scheduling calendar.

What to do

  1. Write down the final schedule submission deadline.
  2. Write down your site’s internal draft deadline.
  3. Schedule time for counselor/site team review.
  4. Schedule time for corrections after the first review.
  5. Identify who has final approval before the schedule is submitted or published.

What to look for

  • Deadlines that fall before breaks, testing windows, graduations, or staffing changes.
  • Whether your first draft is due earlier than the final district deadline.
  • Who needs to review the schedule before it is considered ready.
Example: If the district needs schedules by Friday, your site draft should be ready several days earlier so counselors, APs, department leads, or office staff have time to review and correct errors.
Common mistake: Waiting until the district deadline to discover students are unscheduled, rooms are double-booked, or teacher assignments are incomplete.
Quick check before moving on: You can answer: “When is my first draft due, who reviews it, and when is the final schedule due?”

Step 2 — Understand your bell schedule

Purpose: The bell schedule controls when classes can meet. If the bell schedule is unclear, the master schedule will break later.

Where to go in Aeries

School Info / Scheduling → Bell Schedule / Calendar / Terms

If your district manages bell schedules centrally, you may need to view rather than edit this information.

What to click or check

  1. Open the area where bell schedules, periods, terms, or calendars are stored.
  2. Confirm the number of instructional periods in the day.
  3. Confirm start and end times for each period.
  4. Confirm lunch periods, passing periods, advisory, flex, WIN, or intervention blocks.
  5. Confirm whether the site uses a traditional, block, rotating, flex, or hybrid schedule.
  6. Confirm which terms are used: yearlong, semester, trimester, quarter, or custom terms.

What to look for

  • Periods that do not exist in Aeries but appear on the bell schedule.
  • Lunch or advisory periods that are not scheduled consistently.
  • Flex/WIN periods that need a supervision or attendance plan.
  • Term dates that do not match how courses are offered.
Example: If your school has a 7-period day but a teacher only teaches periods 1–5, you need to know that before assigning sections. If you place that teacher in period 6 or 7 by mistake, the conflict may not appear until later.
Tip: Print or save the bell schedule and keep it open while building. It becomes your map for every section, lunch, prep, flex block, and shared staff assignment.
Common mistake: Adding sections before confirming whether advisory, flex, or intervention is treated as a real scheduled period.
Quick check before moving on: You can explain how many periods your school has, which periods are instructional, and where flex/advisory/intervention fits.

Step 3 — Review enrollment projections

Purpose: Enrollment projections help you estimate how many sections you need. If projections are wrong or missing, you may create too few or too many classes.

Where to go in Aeries

Reports → Enrollment Reports / Student Lists / Grade-Level Counts

Some districts provide projections outside of Aeries. Use the official projection source your district expects.

What to click or check

  1. Run or open enrollment counts by grade level.
  2. For secondary schools, also review course request counts if they are available.
  3. Write down projected enrollment for each grade.
  4. Compare projected counts to current staffing.
  5. Identify grades or courses that may need extra sections.

How to estimate sections

Use a simple formula:

Projected students ÷ target class size = estimated sections
Example: If Grade 7 has 142 projected students and your target class size is 28, then 142 ÷ 28 = 5.07. You should plan for about 6 sections or verify whether 5 larger sections are acceptable under your local staffing/class-size rules.

What to look for

  • Large grade-level increases or decreases.
  • Courses with more requests than seats.
  • Grade levels where class sizes may exceed targets.
  • Programs that require smaller sections.
Common mistake: Using last year’s section counts without checking this year’s projected enrollment.
Quick check before moving on: You have a rough section estimate by grade level or by course.

Step 4 — Review staffing, teacher loads, and FTE

Purpose: Staffing determines what you can actually offer. You cannot build a clean schedule if teacher availability, preps, part-time status, or shared staff are not clear.

Where to go in Aeries

Scheduling → Teachers / Staff Assignments / Master Schedule / Sections

What to click or check

  1. Open your teacher/staff assignment list or scheduling teacher setup.
  2. Confirm each teacher who will appear in the master schedule.
  3. Confirm each teacher’s available periods.
  4. Confirm part-time teachers, shared teachers, itinerant staff, and teachers with special assignments.
  5. Confirm the number of sections or periods each teacher should teach.
  6. Check whether credentials or assignments limit what the teacher can teach.

What to look for

  • Teachers assigned too many sections.
  • Teachers assigned during unavailable periods.
  • Shared teachers scheduled at two sites at the same time.
  • Missing prep periods.
  • Teachers assigned to courses they should not teach.
Example: A part-time teacher who only works mornings should not be placed in an afternoon singleton course. That one placement can create student conflicts and staffing issues.
Tip: Make a simple staffing grid before entering or changing sections: teacher name, course(s), available periods, room, number of sections, special constraints.
Common mistake: Forgetting shared or itinerant staff until after students are scheduled.
Quick check before moving on: You can identify who teaches what, when they are available, and any staff who cannot be moved freely.

Step 5 — Identify protected programs and immovable constraints

Purpose: Some courses, programs, rooms, and staff cannot move easily. Identify them early so the rest of the schedule can be built around them.

Where to go in Aeries

Scheduling → Courses / Sections / Rooms / Programs / Student Groups

What to click or check

  1. Review programs that need specific timing: SPED, ELD, intervention, counseling, PE, music, CTE, labs, AP/Honors, or specialized supports.
  2. Review rooms that are limited: labs, gyms, music rooms, kitchens, shops, learning centers, counseling spaces, or shared rooms.
  3. Review staff who are difficult to move: shared staff, part-time staff, itinerant staff, or teachers with limited availability.
  4. Mark singleton courses or single-section programs.
  5. Build these into the draft before flexible electives or easily moved sections.

What to look for

  • Courses offered only once.
  • Services that must happen at certain times.
  • Rooms that can only hold one specialized course at a time.
  • Students who need both a required course and a protected support class.
Example: If band is only offered period 2 and intervention math is only offered period 2, students who need both cannot be scheduled into both. One singleton conflict can affect many students.
Common mistake: Treating singleton classes like flexible electives.
Quick check before moving on: You have a list of “do not accidentally conflict” courses, staff, rooms, and programs.

Step 6 — Review student requests or student needs

Purpose: Student requests tell you what students need or want. If requests are missing or unrealistic, the schedule will not run cleanly.

Where to go in Aeries

Scheduling → Course Requests / Student Requests / Request Reports

What to click or check

  1. Open course requests or request reports.
  2. Review missing requests by grade level.
  3. Review students with too few or too many requests.
  4. Review duplicate requests.
  5. Review required courses first, then electives or alternates.
  6. For high school, review graduation-critical requests for seniors and credit-deficient students.

What to look for

  • Students missing required courses.
  • Students with no alternate electives.
  • Requests for courses not being offered.
  • Requests for courses that require prerequisites.
  • Too many students requesting a course with too few planned sections.
Tip: Do not wait until after mass scheduling to clean requests. Bad requests create bad schedules.
Quick check before moving on: Requests are complete enough to estimate sections and identify likely conflicts.

Step 7 — Build a first draft in the right order

Purpose: The order you build matters. Build the least flexible items first, then add flexible sections around them.

Where to go in Aeries

Scheduling → Master Schedule / Scheduling Master Schedule / Sections

What to click or do

  1. Start with required courses and protected programs.
  2. Add singleton courses early.
  3. Add courses with limited rooms or limited staff.
  4. Add core sections needed by most students.
  5. Add electives and flexible courses.
  6. Assign teacher, room, period, term, and seat count for each section.
  7. Save and review before mass scheduling students.

What to look for

  • Missing teacher assignments.
  • Missing rooms.
  • Sections in the wrong term.
  • Too few seats for high-demand courses.
  • Single-period conflicts between required courses.
Common mistake: Building all electives first because they feel easier. Required and protected courses should drive the schedule.
Quick check before moving on: Every draft section has a course, teacher, period, term, room if applicable, and reasonable seat count.

Step 8 — Run validation checks before moving to school-specific work

Purpose: Validation catches major problems before you invest too much time in individual fixes.

Where to go in Aeries

Scheduling → Reports / Conflict Reports / Teacher Load / Room Reports / Student Scheduling

What to click or check

  1. Run a teacher load or teacher schedule report.
  2. Run a room schedule report if rooms are used.
  3. Run conflict or unscheduled student reports.
  4. Review overloaded sections and underfilled sections.
  5. Review students with missing periods or unscheduled requests.
  6. Make corrections, then run reports again.

What to look for

  • Many students unscheduled because of the same conflict.
  • Teachers assigned to two classes in one period.
  • Rooms used by two classes in one period.
  • Courses with demand but no available seats.
  • Required courses offered in too few periods.
Tip: Fix patterns before fixing individual students. A single section move can solve dozens of student conflicts.
Quick check before moving on: You have run basic validation reports and know your biggest scheduling problems.
