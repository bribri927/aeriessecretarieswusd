How To Start Master Scheduling

A practical first-steps page for principals and counselors who need to remember where to begin before building or reviewing a master schedule.

Before You Begin

  • District/site scheduling deadlines confirmed.
  • Bell schedule or proposed bell schedule available.
  • Staffing/FTE reviewed with site and district leadership.
  • Room inventory and shared spaces reviewed.
  • Course offerings or grade-level section needs identified.
  • Programs requiring protected time identified: SPED, ELD, intervention, CTE, AP, music, PE, counseling, support services.
  • Person responsible for final submission identified.

Step-by-Step How To Start

Step 1 — Confirm your timeline

Write down when the draft schedule, review cycle, and final version are due.

Tip: Work backward from the final due date. Put internal site deadlines at least one week earlier.

Step 2 — Confirm the bell schedule

Make sure instructional minutes, lunches, passing periods, advisory/flex, and special schedules are clear before sections are built.

Common mistake: Building sections before the bell schedule is stable.

Step 3 — Confirm staffing

Review who is teaching, what they can teach, shared staff constraints, preps, part-time schedules, and special assignments.

Step 4 — Review enrollment projections

Use projections to estimate sections. Divide projected students by target class size, then round up.

Example: 88 students ÷ 28 target = 3.14, so plan 4 sections.

Step 5 — Identify protected programs

Mark courses, programs, or staff that cannot move easily. Build around these early.

Step 6 — Build a first draft

Start with required courses and immovable constraints. Add flexible items later.

Step 7 — Validate before sharing

Run checks for teacher load, rooms, unscheduled students, missing requests, overloaded classes, and incomplete assignments.
